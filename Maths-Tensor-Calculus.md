## [02 - Euclidian Space - Tensor Calculus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2owBYBYc-dc&list=PLXPyU0M9IEAbIYz6O5v3IynmnNlY-uraS&index=2) 

> Euclidean space has the following properties:

- There is no preferred origin (homogeneous).
- There is no preferred direction (isotropic).
- There is no specific way to define a point at infinity.
- Euclidean space is flat.
- Euclidean space is linear.
- Euclidean space is continuous (differentiable).
- The 'metric' that defines the distance between any points p and q (as vectors) is |p-q|^2 = (p-q) * (p-q)

### What does it mean that the euclidean space is flat?

Euclidean space being "flat" means it follows the rules of classical Euclidean geometry, where the space 
**has zero intrinsic curvature**. 

In this, 3D space:

- The shortest path between two points is a straight line
- The Pythagorean theorem holds true.
- Parallel lines never meet, neither converging nor diverging.
- The interior angles of any triangle, no matter how large, sum exactly to pi (180 deg)
- Uniformity: The space is uniform, meaning it has no inherent curvature, unlike the surface of a sphere (positively curved) or a saddle (negatively curved). 

### What does it mean that the euclidean space is linear?

That Euclidean space is linear meansit possesses the structure of a vector space (or linear space), 
allowing vectors (points) to be added together and multiplied by scalars (numbers) while obeying 
specific algebraic rules. 

Like vectors, tensors **are geometric objects**, in particular we say that vectors are tensors of **rank** 1,
scalars are tensors of rank 0 and any tensor is a **geometric object of rank r <= N** whre N is the dimension 
of the **geometric space (manifold)**.

All tensors, therefore including vectors, can be **decomposed into their components with respect a particular**
**choice of the coordinate system in the manifold**. The values of the components are dependant on the choice
of the coordinate system but the geometric tensor is not, it is **(a geometric) invariant** with respect to this choice. 

### Invariant Number of Components of any Tensor:

Any vector in a N-dimensional manifold can be decomposed into its N components.
The generalization of this observation to tensors is the following. 
**Any tensor of rank r <= N in a N-dimensional manifold can be decomposed into its N^r components**.

> Definitions: 

Tensor calculus (aka Tensor Analysis) is an extension of vector calculus.
In contrast to infinitesimal calculus, tensor calculus allows the 
presentetion of phisical equation independent of the choice of a 
particular coordinate system on the manifold.

```
The central idea of tensor calculus is to acknowledge the need for coordinate systems and, 
at the same time, to avoid selecting a particular coordinate system for as long as possible 
in the course of solving a problem.

Tensor calculus provides algorithms for constructing analytical expressions that are valid 
in all coordinate systems simultaneously.
```

# Tensors FAQ

### Are tensor products useful for anything other than quantum mechanics?

Yes, tensor products are useful in many areas besides quantum mechanics, including various fields of mathematics
such as abstract algebra, algebraic geometry, and differential geometry, as well as in physics for describing 
the relationships between physical quantities in classical mechanics, general relativity, and fluid dynamics, 
and in computer science and machine learning for handling large datasets and complex models.

- Vector Space Structure: 
Points in Euclidean space can be treated as vectors, which can be added together and multiplied by scalars.

- Linear Combinations: 
You can form linear combinations of points, allowing for operations like finding the midpoint or extending a line.

- Vector Operations: 
It supports fundamental operations such as vector addition and scalar multiplication, which are crucial for 
defining geometric concepts like lines, planes, and distances.

- Isomorphism to Rn: 
Any N-dimensional Euclidean space is isomorphic to Rn, meaning it can be represented using Cartesian coordinates, 
and operations are performed component-wise. 

- Inner Product: 
The space is equipped with a positive-defined inner product (like the dot product), which defines lengths (norms) and angles.
